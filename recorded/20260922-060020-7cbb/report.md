## What is happening

Start with the uncomfortable part: none of this research tells you why *your* curve bent, or when. There is no publicly available UK/EU-segmented benchmark for supplement subscription second-order retention from Recharge, Ordergroove, Shopify, Subbly or Subscrybe [c1]. The one vertical-specific number that exists — 86.6% of first-time supplement buyers return for a second order, across ~1,800 brands, renewal cycles July 2025–June 2026 — is aggregate, US-skewed, and has no geographic breakout and no year-over-year comparison [c2]. So you cannot currently tell whether you are tracking a deteriorating category baseline or losing ground on your own.

What is solid: the first reorder is where supplements structurally break. 86.6% versus coffee/tea 99.3%, beauty 97.5%, pet supplies 96.2%, health & wellness 92.9% [c3]. And you are not riding a falling tide — Mintel forecasts 10% UK value growth across 2023–2028, and the top five brands' share of mainstream VMS fell from 44% to 40% in two years as challengers gained [c4]. Nor is the consumer broke: UK confidence hit a two-year high in August 2026 at -14, up 11 points year-on-year, with the Major Purchase Index at its strongest since December 2021 [c5].

The research contradicts itself here and I won't smooth it. The trends analyst builds a churn mechanism on affordability — 40% of VMS users stopped or cut back for financial reasons, 52% of consumers cut non-essential spend [c6]. But that evidence is from 2023 and 2024; the confidence data is August 2026. The disagreement is probably about time, not fact. One real caveat in the recent data: the Savings Index fell 5 points to +22 [c7].

## Most likely causes, ranked

**1. Your offer and the second-charge experience are behind the shelf.** This has the most evidence and the most levers. Competitors default to 60–90 day packs — Bare Biology 180 caps ≈2 months at £34.99, Futuro Labs 180 caps as a 90-day supply at £19.99, DR.VEGAN's cheapest tier 3 pouches every 90 days [c8] — which removes two or three churn decision points a year. Subscription discounts have moved past the 15% norm: DR.VEGAN tiers 15/17.5/20%, Feel runs 23% monthly or 35% on 3-packs [c9]. The strongest players add non-price assets — Feel's health portal, points and tenure gifts; DR.VEGAN free in-house nutritionist consultations; BetterYou's BetterRewards [c10]. Meanwhile the dominant Trustpilot complaint in this set is not price but silence before the charge and cancellations that don't take effect [c11]. Heavy first-order discounting is normal (Feel 50% off month one; BetterYou showing 33–47% off) and trains buyers to read full-price order 2 as a price rise [c12], against a published 22–25p/day anchor that premium brands sit 2–4x above [c13].

**2. An expectation gap you are not allowed to close.** Magnesium topped Holland & Barrett's 2025 searches, with glycinate up 180% on the back of the TikTok "sleepy girl mocktail" [c14]. But EFSA authorises no sleep claim — only muscle and nerve function, psychological function, tiredness and fatigue, energy metabolism and bones [c15]. Buyers now shop by chemical form, searching glycinate, threonate and citrate by name [c16]. You recruited on a promise you may not make, into a market where switching is cheap.

**3. Tolerability — real, but much smaller than the first pass implied.** The follow-up work demotes this. In the only well-powered head-to-head (450 mg elemental, 24 weeks), 4.3% discontinued, and citrate caused more discontinuations (2/46) than oxide (0/46) [c17]. Cochrane found diarrhoea in 11–37% on magnesium versus 10–14% on control, but withdrawal rates no different from placebo [c18]. At 250 mg bisglycinate over 28 days, 10.4% withdrew versus 16.7% on placebo, none GI-attributed [c19]. Bisglycinate is not clean at 375 mg — it caused the only adverse-event dropout in a four-form crossover [c20]. And the 250-vs-375 "contradiction" isn't one: 250 mg is the EU supplemental upper level, 375 mg is a labelling reference value with no safety meaning, and the UK uses a separate 400 mg guidance level [c21]. BfR advises splitting the dose [c22], but split dosing has never been tested against single dosing for GI symptoms or discontinuation [c23].

**4. EU duty — demote to an operational check.** The flat €3 duty per 4-digit tariff heading began 1 July 2026 [c24], but liability sits with the declarant, not the consumer [c25], and a single-SKU supplement order normally attracts one €3 charge [c26]. The ~€2 handling fee was not in force before ~1 November 2026 [c27], and no source reports any dated step change in UK→EU repeat rates in July 2026 [c28]. Whether your customer gets billed depends on your own carrier setup: IOSS covers VAT only, so on duty-unpaid lanes the carrier gates delivery with a payment request [c29]. PPWR packaging obligations from 12 August 2026 confound the same window anyway [c30].

## What this means for positioning

Stop defending a monthly full-price charge and start selling a 60–90 day regimen with a tiered commitment discount. Make the thing you *can* claim — tiredness and fatigue, muscle and nerve function — the spine of the onboarding, and let form (bisglycinate, specified dose, split-dose instruction) carry the differentiation that sleep language legally cannot. Treat pre-billing reminders, one-click cancellation and removable billing details as marketing assets, not compliance chores. DMCC commencement is genuinely disputed across autumn 2026, January 2027 and spring 2027 [c31], but the direction is fixed: reminder notices arrive, and any retention resting on inertia gets reclassified as churn [c32].

## What to test next

1. Ask Recharge (which now owns Skio) for a region-filtered cut: UK/EU supplement first-reorder rate, 2024 vs 2025 vs 2026. The data exists; it is simply never published by geography [c1].
2. Date your own decline. If it is a step change, look at a pricing, pack, shipping or carrier change on that date. If it is a drift, it is competitive.
3. Pull your EU carrier config by destination and check which lanes are duty-unpaid. That is a contract question you can answer today.
4. Run a 90-day pack with a 20%+ tiered discount against your current 30-day, and separately test a pre-billing reminder at T-5 days. Both are cheap and independent.
5. Test split-dose instruction on-pack. Zero evidence it works, near-zero cost.

## What we could not verify

Whether the UK/EU baseline is flat or falling — unevidenced in either direction [c1]. The global signal mildly favours deterioration: health & wellness monthly churn went from ~4.2% in 2023 to 8.8% and rising in 2026 [c33], but monthly churn and order-1→2 conversion are different metrics [c34]. No study anywhere reports magnesium discontinuation at 30–60 days split by form and dose [c35]. And the popular claim that bisglycinate is significantly better tolerated could not be traced to any primary source [c36].
