## What is happening

Germany is the right category for you and the wrong playbook.

Roasted coffee volume fell 1.5% to 456,000 tonnes in 2025 while revenue rose 23.5% to €8.98bn — the growth was price, not demand [c1]. Inside that flat market, whole bean hit a record 193,800 tonnes, up 130% in ten years, while roast & ground fell roughly 38% [c2]. One in three households owns a bean-to-cup machine [c3], average household specialty spend rose from €43 in 2020 to €58 in 2023 [c4], and 83% of the market was certified sustainable by 2022 — so certification is table stakes, not a story [c5]. Out-of-home consumption also set a record 125,500 tonnes, meaning you compete with the café occasion too [c6].

The buyer is cautious rather than broke: GfK/NIM consumer climate stood at -26.6 in September 2026, with income expectations turning positive (+1.7) for the first time in six months but willingness to buy flat at -9.8 and savings propensity elevated at 15.5 [c7]. And 39% of German consumers have already cancelled or cut online subscriptions, with 1.28 per person queued for cancellation [c8].

## What most likely decides the outcome, ranked

**1. Where you roast and ship from (highest impact, hardest deadline).** The EU abolishes the €150 duty exemption on 1 July 2026, adding a flat €3 per 4-digit tariff heading per consignment plus an e-commerce handling fee from November 2026 [c9]. Worse, a UK-dispatched parcel is a third-country import: excise, import VAT and a customs declaration arise per consignment with the declarant as debtor [c10]. From EU stock the picture is manageable: §18 KaffeeStG makes the distance seller liable for €2.19/kg on roasted coffee, arising on delivery to the German consumer [c11]; since 1 July 2021 the seller obtains the Hauptzollamt permit itself and a resident Steuervertreter is optional [c12]; security is required but can be waived or set at one month's tax under a monthly-declaration simplification [c13]; and retail beans carry 7% VAT, not 19% [c14]. This resolves the contradiction in our earlier reading — Stripe's claim that direct-to-consumer EU shipments are exempt is wrong, and the "mandatory German representative" guidance is pre-2021. The excise is €0.55 on a 250g bag, not 10% of revenue unless you ship ~1kg boxes [c15].

**2. The payment stack.** Cards are only 13.7% of German online payments against PayPal at 28.7%, invoice 26.1% and direct debit 14.4% [c16], and 82% of German shoppers abandon when their method is missing [c17]. SEPA Direct Debit is the best recurring rail but carries an unconditional 8-week refund right and 13 months for unauthorised debits [c18]; as a UK entity you need a euro IBAN and must pass full debtor name and address or face declines [c19]. PayPal reference transactions — the only way to charge a billing agreement unattended — are limited-release and generally reserved for merchants with PayPal history you don't have [c20], and a customer-side revocation is terminal and cannot be reactivated [c21]. Klarna can do recurring invoice via a customer token, but re-underwrites every renewal with no step-up on failure and must be activated on your MID [c22]. Billie is B2B-only [c23].

**3. Every retention lever you'd use is already conceded.** Tchibo runs a permanent 10% (15% bulk, 20% XL), free shipping, ~90 coffees, a €29 minimum and no minimum term [c24], plus up to 100% off a machine against 4 deliveries (6 for capsules) [c25]. Coffee Circle offers 10% from delivery one, 250g at €10.90 (€43.60/kg), free shipping over €39, eleven intervals from 2–16 weeks, self-serve pause and 21,740 reviews at 4.91/5 [c26]. roastmarket monetises loyalty at €19.90/year rather than locking customers in [c27]. Beanz by Breville already runs a German storefront with 100+ specialty coffees dispatched by the roaster [c28].

**4. The law removes the fallback.** §312k BGB has required a permanently visible, login-free cancellation button since 1 July 2022 [c29]; the 2025 BGH ruling against Otto barred login walls, multi-page flows, forms and a pause-prompt on the cancellation form [c30]; and under §312k(6) a non-compliant button lets customers terminate at any time [c31]. From 19 June 2026 §356a adds a withdrawal button binding on non-German traders targeting Germany, with an unstarted 14-day clock — effectively indefinite withdrawal — as the penalty [c32]. Our sources disagree on the exact mandated label ("Verträge hier kündigen" vs "Jetzt kündigen"); get German counsel to fix the string before you build.

**5. Compliance gates that bite before revenue.** LUCID/VerpackG registration is required before your first German sale with no threshold, fines to €200,000 and a sales ban [c33]; PPWR applies from 12 August 2026 [c34]; EUDR applies to coffee from 30 December 2026, with micro/small operators getting until 30 June 2027 [c35].

## What this means for positioning

Pick your anchor explicitly. Tchibo's 500g sits near €19.98/kg [c37] and 19grams' specialty entry is €12.90–14.90/month for 250g [c36]; £18 (~€21) is above entry specialty but below one Coffee Circle bag-month. Note the research contradicts itself here: Coffee Circle reads as €43.60/kg on one page and €59.90/kg on another [c38], which moves £18 from "sub-scale" to "at market." Also drop the "everything is inflating anyway" cover — bean coffee was +12.2% y/y in April 2025 [c39], but by mid-2026 coffee was +5.6% y/y, fell 2.3% month-on-month in July, arabica was down 37.8% from its October 2025 record and a ~10m bag surplus is forecast [c40].

The durable opening is operational: German complaints cluster on wrong deliveries and unresolved service cases, not coffee quality [c41], and enthusiasts object to subscriptions on restriction, valuing roast-date transparency and month-to-month flexibility [c42]. Position on freshness dating, roaster-level variety and fulfilment accuracy — and price pickup-point delivery in, since DHL raised business parcel rates in January 2026 and 41,000+ pickup points are a mainstream expectation [c43].

## What to test next

Run a 500-subscriber German pilot off EU stock before committing capital. Test: (1) €14.90 vs €19.90 vs €24.90 at 250g and 2×250g, against both anchors; (2) SEPA vs PayPal vs Klarna-invoice-then-SEPA on conversion *and* renewal success; (3) the §312k/§356a-compliant funnel's effect on trial-to-month-three; (4) roast-date-on-pack as the headline claim; (5) pickup-point vs home delivery on cost and complaints.

## What we could not verify

Usable German involuntary-churn and Rücklastschrift benchmarks do not exist — published figures range from under 0.1% to about 4.5%, with the German e-commerce number tracing to pre-SEPA material [c44]. The "37% of 18–29s cancelled since December 2025" stat has no supporting age breakdown in the underlying October 2024 study [c45]. Beanz' 4.3/5 Trustpilot score comes from its US profile, not Germany [c46]. And 19grams markets "ohne Mindestlaufzeit" while its own Coffee Club page states a 3-month commitment [c47] — worth resolving, because a lawful minimum term would widen your retention options considerably.
